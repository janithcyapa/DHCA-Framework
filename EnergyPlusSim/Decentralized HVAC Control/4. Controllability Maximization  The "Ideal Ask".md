
To maintain thermal comfort and indoor air quality, the Zone Controller must command its local VAV damper flow ($u$) while simultaneously requesting optimal supply conditions ($T_s, W_s, C_s$) from the central Air Handling Unit (AHU). 

The optimal AHU request is termed the **"Ideal Ask."**

## 4.1 The Mathematical Roadblock: Bilinearity

Ideally, we would ask our Quadratic Programming (QP) solver to find the optimal flow ($u$) and the optimal supply temperature ($T_s$) simultaneously. However, the physical dynamics of a thermal zone rely on the energy equation:

$$ \dot{Q} = \rho_{air} c_p u (T_s - T_{in}) $$

Because both the mass flow rate ($u$) and the supply temperature ($T_s$) are unknown decision variables, they multiply against each other ($u \cdot T_s$). This creates a **bilinear** non-linearity. Standard QP solvers (like OSQP) require strictly linear dynamics and will fail if presented with multiplied decision variables.

To bypass this without abandoning the speed and reliability of a linear QP solver, we implement **Sequential (Alternating) Linearization**.

---

## 4.2 The Sequential Linearization Algorithm

Instead of solving one massive, impossible problem, we solve two mathematically perfect, linear problems back-to-back at every timestep. 

### Phase 1: Flow Optimization (Fixed Supply)
We assume the AHU supply conditions will remain exactly what they currently are. 
1. Freeze $v_s = [T_s, W_s, C_s]^T$ as constants.
2. The dynamics are now linear with respect to the VAV flow $u$.
3. Execute the standard Zone MPC QP (as defined in Section 3) to find the optimal airflow trajectory $U^*$.
4. Extract the immediate optimal flow command: $u_0^*$.

### Phase 2: Supply Optimization (Fixed Flow)
Now, we lock the VAV damper at the optimal flow we just calculated ($u_0^*$). By treating the flow as a known constant, the bilinear equation becomes linear with respect to the supply conditions. 
1. Freeze $u = u_0^*$.
2. Define a new decision vector for the AHU supply request: $Z_{ask} = [T_s, W_s, C_s]^T$.
3. Formulate and solve a secondary QP to find the optimal $Z_{ask}^*$. This is our **Ideal Ask**.

---

## 4.3 Formulating the Phase 2 QP

With $u$ frozen at $u_0^*$, we must recalculate the continuous Jacobians for the Zone physics. 

#### The State Matrix ($A_{ask}$)
The state matrix now integrates the known flow rate $u_0^*$ into the system's thermal and mass resistance:

$$A_{ask} = \begin{bmatrix} 
-\frac{1}{C_{air}}\left(\frac{1}{R_{ext}} + \frac{1}{R_{int}} + \rho_{air} c_p u_0^*\right) & \frac{1}{C_{air} R_{int}} & 0 & 0 \\ 
\frac{1}{C_{mass} R_{int}} & -\frac{1}{C_{mass} R_{int}} & 0 & 0 \\ 
0 & 0 & - \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0^*}{C_{air}} & 0 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 & - \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0^*}{C_{air}} 
\end{bmatrix}$$

#### The Input Matrix ($B_{ask}$)
The input matrix now maps how changes in the AHU supply conditions ($T_s, W_s, C_s$) affect the indoor states. This creates a $4 \times 3$ matrix:

$$B_{ask} = \begin{bmatrix} 
\frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0^*}{C_{air}} & 0 & 0 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 \\ 
0 & \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0^*}{C_{air}} & 0 \\ 
0 & 0 & \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0^*}{C_{air}} 
\end{bmatrix}$$

#### The Objective Function
The goal of this secondary QP is to find a supply state that keeps the zone within its comfort bands, but requires the **least thermal effort** from the AHU. 

We define a "neutral" AHU state where the AHU uses zero heating, zero cooling, and zero dehumidification (i.e., recirculating room air). We penalize deviation from this neutral state:

$$ J_{ask} = q_T (T_s - T_{neutral})^2 + q_W (W_s - W_{neutral})^2 + q_C (C_s - C_{neutral})^2 $$

#### The Constraints
We apply the exact same comfort band constraints (via slack variables) used in Phase 1, but we add physical bounds on what the AHU coils can actually achieve:
$$ T_{s,min} \le T_s \le T_{s,max} $$
$$ W_{s,min} \le W_s \le W_{s,max} $$
$$ C_{s,min} \le C_s \le C_{s,max} $$

Solving this secondary QP yields the mathematically optimal request vector $Z_{ask}^* = [T_s^*, W_s^*, C_s^*]^T$. The zone broadcasts this Ideal Ask to the AHU Coordinator.

## 4.4 Alternative Control Architectures (Future Iterations)

While Sequential Linearization is highly effective and allows the use of fast, embedded QP solvers, there are other theoretical approaches to deriving the Ideal Ask.

### Option A: Energy/Mass Flux as the Decision Variable (The Linear Subsumption Method)
Instead of asking the solver to find a physical flow rate ($u$) or a physical temperature ($T_s$), we rewrite the physics equations so the MPC solves directly for the **Total Heat Flux** ($\dot{Q}_{cmd}$) and **Mass Fluxes** ($\dot{m}_{W,cmd}$, $\dot{m}_{C,cmd}$) required by the room.

$$ z = [\dot{Q}_{cmd}, \dot{m}_{W,cmd}, \dot{m}_{C,cmd}]^T $$

Because energy and mass flux are independent scalar variables, the math is perfectly linear and requires only a single pass through a standard QP solver. Once the optimal fluxes are found, a deterministic, post-processing script outside the solver splits the energy into physical commands ($u_{cmd}$ and $T_s^*$) based on the AHU's current operating limits. This is highly elegant but requires re-working the core physical state-space equations.

### Option B: Non-Linear MPC (NMPC)
If computational power is not a constraint, the QP solver can be entirely replaced with a Non-Linear solver (e.g., IPOPT or CasADi). 

In an NMPC architecture, the bilinear equation $\dot{Q} = \rho c_p u (T_s - T_{in})$ is fed to the solver exactly as written. The decision vector contains both the flow and supply conditions simultaneously: $z = [u_0, T_{s,0}, \dots]$. The solver finds the absolute global optimum in a single pass. The primary drawbacks are slower computation times and the risk of the solver failing to converge if it gets trapped in a local minimum.

### Option C: Reinforcement Learning (RL) / Data-Driven Ask
Instead of relying on real-time mathematical optimization, a neural network is trained via Reinforcement Learning (using tools like Proximal Policy Optimization). The RL agent observes the EKF state estimation ($x_0, d_0$) and outputs a policy-driven continuous action vector for the Ideal Ask. 

While RL eliminates solver computation time during live operation (since inferencing a neural network is near-instantaneous), guaranteeing safety constraints (e.g., absolutely never letting the room freeze) is notoriously difficult in purely data-driven control compared to the hard mathematical constraints of an MPC formulation.
