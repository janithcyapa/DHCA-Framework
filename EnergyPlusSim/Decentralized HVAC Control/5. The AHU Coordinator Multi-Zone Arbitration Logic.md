# The AHU Coordinator: Multi-Zone Arbitration Logic

Because the AHU serves multiple independent zones via a single primary duct, it must supply a single, global thermodynamic state $v_s^{AHU} = [T_s^{AHU}, W_s^{AHU}, C_s^{AHU}]^T$.

The Coordinator's objective is to resolve conflicts between individual zone requests (the set of Ideal Asks: $\mathcal{V}^* = \{v_{s,1}^*, v_{s,2}^*, \dots, v_{s,N_z}^*\}$) while minimizing central plant energy. It acts as an instantaneous, closed-form dispatcher (Rules 1–3) followed by a numerical trim loop (Rule 4) ensuring no zone loses controllability.

---

## 9.1 Rule 1: Ventilation (CO₂) Arbitration

Providing outside fresh air is moderately energy-intensive. The AHU must open its outdoor air dampers just enough to satisfy the zone with the *worst* indoor air quality (the one demanding the lowest CO₂ concentration).

$$C_s^{AHU} = \min_{i \in \{1 \dots N_z\}} \big(C_{s,i}^*\big)$$

*Logic: If any single zone requests maximum fresh air ($C_{s,min}$), the AHU must provide it to prevent that zone's CO₂ ceiling constraint from being violated.*

---

## 9.2 Rule 2: Humidity Arbitration

Because $W_{s,i}^*$ takes three possible values — $W_{s,min}$ (dehumidify), $W_{neutral}$ (satisfied), $W_{s,max}$ (humidify), with $W_{s,min} < W_{neutral} < W_{s,max}$ — arbitration requires an explicit priority tier rather than a simple extremum. **Dehumidification is prioritized**: over-humidified air risks condensation and mold growth, a more severe outcome than the comfort cost of under-humidified air.

$$W_{demand} = \begin{cases} W_{s,min}, & \text{if } \exists\, i \text{ such that } W_{s,i}^{*} = W_{s,min} & \text{(dehumidification priority)} \\[4pt] W_{s,max}, & \text{if no dehumidification requests, and } \exists\, i \text{ such that } W_{s,i}^{*} = W_{s,max} \\[4pt] W_{neutral}, & \text{otherwise (all zones satisfied)} \end{cases}$$

$$W_s^{AHU} = W_{demand}$$

*Logic: If any zone is saturated and requests $W_{s,min}$, the AHU must wring out the moisture globally. Otherwise, if any zone requests humidification and none require dehumidification, the AHU raises the moisture content globally.*

---

## 9.3 Rule 3: Sensible (Temperature) Arbitration & Psychrometric Coupling

Temperature conflict resolution must resolve opposing requests (e.g., a perimeter zone needing heat vs. an interior zone needing cooling) while respecting the physical coupling between temperature and humidity at the cooling coil.

**Step 3a — Conflict Resolution (Cooling Priority).** In a standard single-duct VAV system, supplying hot air when a zone requires cooling causes a rapid, uncontrollable temperature spike. Over-cooling a zone that requires heat simply drives its VAV box to its minimum damper position. The dispatcher therefore strictly prioritizes cooling requests:

$$T_{demand} = \begin{cases} T_{s,min}, & \text{if } \exists\, i \text{ such that } T_{s,i}^{*} = T_{s,min} \\ T_{s,max}, & \text{if no cooling requests, and } \exists\, i \text{ such that } T_{s,i}^{*} = T_{s,max} \\ T_{neutral}, & \text{otherwise (all zones satisfied)} \end{cases}$$

**Step 3b — Psychrometric Override.** Temperature and humidity are physically coupled at the cooling coil: if the AHU is forced to dehumidify (Rule 2), the supply air temperature cannot be warmer than the saturation (dew point) temperature required to achieve that humidity ratio. Let $T_{sat}(W)$ map humidity ratio to dew point temperature. Define the feasible sensible-supply bound, recomputed every control cycle from the current zone requests and the current $W_s^{AHU}$:

$$T_s^{bound}(t) = \min\Big(T_{demand}(t),\ T_{sat}\big(W_s^{AHU}(t)\big)\Big)$$

This bound is the operating ceiling handed to Rule 4 — it guarantees mathematically optimal alignment for the worst-case zones while leaving room for Rule 4 to trim within it for energy efficiency.

---

## 9.4 Rule 4: Global Energy Optimization (The Fan vs. Chiller vs. Boiler Trade-off)

Once Rules 1–3 establish the controllability bounds, the Coordinator finds the specific setpoint within those bounds that minimizes total central plant energy.

**The Energy Cost Function.** Total instantaneous AHU power is the sum of fan, chiller, and boiler power:

$$P_{AHU} = P_{fan} + P_{chiller} + P_{boiler}$$

$$P_{fan} = k_{fan} \left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_z} u_i\right)^{3}$$

$$P_{chiller} = \frac{\rho_{air}}{\text{COP}} \left(\sum_i u_i\right) \Big(h_{mix} - h_{supply}(T_s, W_s)\Big)$$

$$P_{boiler} = \frac{\rho_{air}}{\eta_{boiler}} \left(\sum_i u_i\right) \max\Big(0,\ h_{supply}(T_s, W_s) - h_{mix}\Big)$$

where $\eta_{boiler}$ is boiler thermal efficiency. $P_{boiler}$ is active only on the rare branch where $T_{demand} = T_{s,max}$ (no zone requests cooling); in a tropical-climate deployment this term will typically be near zero but is retained for completeness.

Because required zone airflow $u_i$ is a function of $T_s$ (warmer supply air needs more volume for the same cooling), a convex trade-off exists between fan and chiller power. The optimization problem is:

$$\min_{T_s} \quad P_{AHU}(T_s) \qquad \text{s.t.} \qquad T_{s,min} \le T_s \le T_s^{bound}(t), \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{N_z} u_i(T_s) \le \dot{V}_{fan,max}$$

**Implementation via Trim and Respond (ASHRAE Guideline 36).** Solved as a numerical gradient loop rather than in closed form. Let $u_{max,i}$ be the maximum physical airflow for zone $i$'s VAV box, and let $T_s^{opt}(t)$ be the persistent, slowly-adjusted broadcast setpoint (distinct from the freshly-recomputed bound $T_s^{bound}(t)$).

**Step 1 — Zone Saturation Index**, from the previous MPC step's damper positions:

$$I_{max} = \max_{i} \left(\frac{u_{i,k-1}}{u_{max,i}}\right)$$

**Step 2 — Gradient Update Law**, clamped on both sides against the current bound on every branch:

$$T_s^{opt}(t) = \begin{cases} \max\Big(T_s^{opt}(t-1) - \Delta T_{step},\ T_{s,min}\Big), & I_{max} > 95\% \quad \text{(zone starving for cooling — chiller takes the load)} \\[6pt] \min\Big(T_s^{opt}(t-1) + \Delta T_{step},\ T_s^{bound}(t)\Big), & I_{max} < 85\% \quad \text{(fan capacity to spare — shift load from chiller to fan)} \\[6pt] \operatorname{clip}\Big(T_s^{opt}(t-1),\ T_{s,min},\ T_s^{bound}(t)\Big), & \text{otherwise (hold)} \end{cases}$$

The hold branch is re-clipped against $T_s^{bound}(t)$ on every cycle — not just passed through — because $T_{demand}$ (Step 3a) is a bang-bang signal that can tighten abruptly the instant a new zone's error crosses zero, even on cycles where the saturation index itself doesn't trigger a trim step.

This drives the AHU to the optimal point on the $P_{AHU}$ curve without a centralized non-linear solver, fully integrating the Zone MPC's flow minimization with AHU-level thermodynamic efficiency, while guaranteeing $T_s^{opt}(t) \in [T_{s,min}, T_s^{bound}(t)]$ at every timestep.

---

## 9.5 Execution Order

At every AHU control cycle:

1. Collect Ideal Asks $\{v_{s,i}^*\}$ from all zones.
2. **Rule 1:** $C_s^{AHU} = \min_i(C_{s,i}^*)$.
3. **Rule 2:** compute $W_{demand}$ (§9.2) → $W_s^{AHU} = W_{demand}$.
4. **Rule 3:** compute $T_{demand}$, then $T_s^{bound}(t) = \min\big(T_{demand}, T_{sat}(W_s^{AHU}(t))\big)$.
5. **Rule 4:** update $T_s^{opt}(t)$ via the gradient law in §9.4.
6. Broadcast $v_s^{AHU}(t) = \big[T_s^{opt}(t),\ W_s^{AHU}(t),\ C_s^{AHU}(t)\big]^T$ to all zone MPCs.
