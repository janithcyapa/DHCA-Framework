
## The Control Philosophy

Unlike traditional MPC which minimizes state-tracking errors $(x_k - x_{ref})^2$, this Economic/Deadband MPC removes state tracking entirely from the objective function. The objective is strictly to **minimize the VAV flow rate (**$u$**) and its rate of change (**$\Delta u$**)**, subject to keeping the states inside a specified band. To prevent solver failure (infeasibility) during extreme disturbances, the state bounds are formulated as **soft constraints** heavily penalized by slack variables ($\epsilon$).

### Objective Function (Cost)

$$J = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \Big( r \cdot u_k^2 + r_\Delta \cdot (u_k - u_{k-1})^2 \Big) + \sum_{k=1}^{N} \Big( \lambda_T \epsilon_{T,k}^2 + \lambda_W \epsilon_{W,k}^2 + \lambda_C \epsilon_{C,k}^2 \Big)$$

Where $r$ penalizes absolute fan power, $r_\Delta$ prevents actuator oscillation, and $\lambda \gg r$ strictly enforces the comfort bands unless physically impossible.

## Linearization 
### The Taylor Series Expansion (The Operating Point)

To linearize, we take the first-order Taylor Series expansion around a specific operating point. In Successive Linearization MPC, the operating point is the current known reality:

- $x_0$: The current state estimated by your EKF.
- $u_0$: The previous control action applied ($u_{-1}$).
- $d_0$: The current disturbance estimated by your EKF.

The Taylor expansion gives us:
$$\dot{x} \approx f(x_0, u_0, d_0) + \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\bigg\vert{}_{op} (x - x_0) + \frac{\partial f}{\partial u}\bigg\vert{}_{op} (u - u_0)$$

Where $A_c = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $B_c = \frac{\partial f}{\partial u}$.

### Deriving the Continuous Jacobians ($A_c$ and $B_c$)

Let's take the partial derivatives of the four equations with respect to the four states to build the $4 \times 4$ State Matrix ($A_c$).

**Row 1 (Derivatives of $f_1$):**
$$A_{c(1,1)} = \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial T_{in}} = - \frac{1}{C_{air} R_{ext}} - \frac{1}{C_{air} R_{int}} - \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0}{C_{air}}$$
$$A_{c(1,2)} = \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial T_m} = \frac{1}{C_{air} R_{int}}$$
**Row 2 (Derivatives of $f_2$):**
$$A_{c(2,1)} = \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial T_{in}} = \frac{1}{C_{mass} R_{int}}$$
$$A_{c(2,2)} = \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial T_m} = - \frac{1}{C_{mass} R_{int}}$$
**Row 3 (Derivatives of $f_3$):**
$$A_{c(3,3)} = \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial W_{in}} = - \frac{c_p \rho_{air} u_0}{C_{air}}$$
**Row 4 (Derivatives of $f_4$):**
$$A_{c(4,4)} = \frac{\partial f_4}{\partial C_{in}} = - \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0}{C_{air}}$$

Putting it all together, your continuous state matrix is mostly diagonal:

$$A_c = \begin{bmatrix} -\frac{1}{C_{air}}\left(\frac{1}{R_{ext}} + \frac{1}{R_{int}} + \rho_{air} c_p u_0\right) & \frac{1}{C_{air} R_{int}} & 0 & 0 \\ \frac{1}{C_{mass} R_{int}} & -\frac{1}{C_{mass} R_{int}} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & - \frac{c_p \rho_{air} u_0}{C_{air}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & - \frac{\rho_{air} c_p u_0}{C_{air}} \end{bmatrix}$$

Now, we take the partial derivatives with respect to the control input $u$ to build the $4 \times 1$ Input Matrix ($B_c$).

$$B_c = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial u} \\ \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial u} \\ \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial u} \\ \frac{\partial f_4}{\partial u} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\rho_{air} c_p}{C_{air}} (T_s - T_{in,0}) \\ 0 \\ \frac{c_p \rho_{air}}{C_{air}} (W_s - W_{in,0}) \\ \frac{\rho_{air} c_p}{C_{air}} (C_s - C_{in,0}) \end{bmatrix}$$

### 4. Rearranging into Affine Form

Go back to the Taylor expansion equation and group the constants together:
$$\dot{x} \approx f(x_0, u_0, d_0) + A_c x - A_c x_0 + B_c u - B_c u_0$$
$$\dot{x} \approx A_c x + B_c u + \underbrace{\Big[ f(x_0, u_0, d_0) - A_c x_0 - B_c u_0 \Big]}_{c_c}$$

Here, $c_c$ is the continuous-time affine offset vector. It perfectly compensates for the difference between the linearized tangent plane and the true non-linear curve at your operating point, preventing steady-state errors.

### Forward Euler Discretization

A standard QP solver cannot integrate continuous time ($\dot{x}$). We must map these matrices across your sampling time interval $\Delta t$ to predict the next discrete step $x_{k+1}$.

Using the Forward Euler approximation:
$$x_{k+1} \approx x_k + \dot{x}_k \Delta t$$
Substitute our affine formulation into this:
$$x_{k+1} = x_k + (A_c x_k + B_c u_k + c_c) \Delta t$$
$$x_{k+1} = (I + A_c \Delta t) x_k + (B_c \Delta t) u_k + (c_c \Delta t)$$
This leaves you with your final, linearized, discrete-time matrices ready to be plugged into the $\Psi$ and $\Theta$ prediction matrices you outlined in your MPC formulation:

**1. Discrete State Matrix:**
$$A_d = I_{4 \times 4} + (A_c \cdot \Delta t)$$
**2. Discrete Input Matrix:**
$$B_d = B_c \cdot \Delta t$$
**3. Discrete Affine Offset:**
$$c_d = \Big[ f(x_0, u_0, d_0) - A_c x_0 - B_c u_0 \Big] \cdot \Delta t$$
The discrete linear update used for the MPC prediction horizon is,
$$x_{k+1} = A_d x_k + B_d u_k + c_d$$
## State Prediction Over Horizon $N$

We map the entire horizon into dense matrices. Let $X$ be the concatenated state vector and $U$ be the concatenated input vector:

$$X = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_N \end{bmatrix}_{4N \times 1}, \quad U = \begin{bmatrix} u_0 \\ u_1 \\ \vdots \\ u_{N-1} \end{bmatrix}_{N \times 1}$$

The prediction equation over the horizon becomes:

$$X = \Psi x_0 + \Theta U + \Phi c_d$$

Where:

$$\Psi = \begin{bmatrix} A_d \\ A_d^2 \\ \vdots \\ A_d^N \end{bmatrix}, \quad \Theta = \begin{bmatrix} B_d & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ A_d B_d & B_d & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ A_d^{N-1} B_d & A_d^{N-2} B_d & \dots & B_d \end{bmatrix}, \quad \Phi = \begin{bmatrix} I \\ I + A_d \\ \vdots \\ \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} A_d^i \end{bmatrix}$$

## 4. Constraint Formulation

### Extracting Specific States

We define extraction matrices $S_T, S_W, S_C$ of size $(N \times 4N)$ to pull only the relevant states from $X$:
$$S_T = I_N \otimes [1, 0, 0, 0] \quad (\text{Extracts } T_{in})$$$$S_W = I_N \otimes [0, 0, 1, 0] \quad (\text{Extracts } W_{in})$$$$S_C = I_N \otimes [0, 0, 0, 1] \quad (\text{Extracts } C_{in})$$
This allows us to write the predicted trajectories as affine functions of $U$. Let:

- $F_T = S_T \Theta$ and $g_T = S_T (\Psi x_0 + \Phi c_d)$
- $F_W = S_W \Theta$ and $g_W = S_W (\Psi x_0 + \Phi c_d)$
- $F_C = S_C \Theta$ and $g_C = S_C (\Psi x_0 + \Phi c_d)$

So, the predicted temperature trajectory is simply: $T_{1:N} = F_T U + g_T$

### Formulating the Soft Bounds

Let $\epsilon_T, \epsilon_W, \epsilon_C$ be column vectors of length $N$. For Temperature ($T_{min} \le T_{1:N} \le T_{max}$):
$$F_T U + g_T - \epsilon_T \le T_{max} \implies F_T U - \epsilon_T \le T_{max} - g_T$$$$-(F_T U + g_T) - \epsilon_T \le -T_{min} \implies -F_T U - \epsilon_T \le -T_{min} + g_T$$
For Humidity (Two-sided):
$$F_W U - \epsilon_W \le W_{max} - g_W$$$$-F_W U - \epsilon_W \le -W_{min} + g_W$$
For CO2 (One-sided ceiling):

$$F_C U - \epsilon_C \le C_{max} - g_C$$

## ost Function Assembly ($H$ and $f$)

We pack all decision variables into a single vector $z$ of length $4N$:

$$z = \begin{bmatrix} U \\ \epsilon_T \\ \epsilon_W \\ \epsilon_C \end{bmatrix}$$

### The Rate of Change Penalty ($\Delta U$)

To penalize $(u_k - u_{k-1})^2$, define the difference matrix $D$ ($N \times N$) and vector $E$ ($N \times 1$):

$$D = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & \dots \\ -1 & 1 & 0 & \dots \\ 0 & -1 & 1 & \dots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots \end{bmatrix}, \quad E = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$$$\Delta U = D U - E u_{-1}$$

The input cost is $U^T(R \cdot I)U + (DU - Eu_{-1})^T (R_\Delta \cdot I) (DU - Eu_{-1})$. Expanding this gives the quadratic and linear terms for $U$:

- Quadratic: $U^T (R \cdot I + D^T R_\Delta D) U$
- Linear: $-2 u_{-1} E^T R_\Delta D U$

### Final $H$ and $f$ Matrices

The standard QP objective is $\frac{1}{2} z^T H z + f^T z$. We multiply our penalties by 2 to align with the $\frac{1}{2}$ term.

$$H = 2 \begin{bmatrix} R \cdot I + D^T R_\Delta D & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \Lambda_T \cdot I & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \Lambda_W \cdot I & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \Lambda_C \cdot I \end{bmatrix}$$$$f = \begin{bmatrix} -2 R_\Delta D^T E u_{-1} \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

## Full QP Assembly (Matrix $A_{ineq} \cdot z \le b_{ineq}$)

We stack all constraints into a massive matrix $A_{ineq}$ and vector $b_{ineq}$ to feed directly into OSQP, CVXPY, or quadprog.

$$A_{ineq} = \begin{bmatrix} F_T & -I_N & 0 & 0 \\ -F_T & -I_N & 0 & 0 \\ F_W & 0 & -I_N & 0 \\ -F_W & 0 & -I_N & 0 \\ F_C & 0 & 0 & -I_N \\ 0 & -I_N & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -I_N & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -I_N \\ I_N & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -I_N & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad b_{ineq} = \begin{bmatrix} T_{max} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} - g_T \\ -T_{min} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} + g_T \\ W_{max} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} - g_W \\ -W_{min} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} + g_W \\ C_{max} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} - g_C \\ \mathbf{0}_N \\ \mathbf{0}_N \\ \mathbf{0}_N \\ u_{max} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} \\ -u_{min} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{N} \end{bmatrix}$$

_(Note:_ $\mathbf{1}_{N}$ _and_ $\mathbf{0}_N$ _denote vectors of ones and zeros of length_ $N$_. Rows 6-8 force the slacks_ $\epsilon \ge 0$_. Rows 9-10 enforce physical VAV actuator limits)._

## 7. Execution Loop (Real-Time Implementation)

At every sampling interval $\Delta t$ (e.g., 60 seconds):

1. **Estimate states and disturbances** $(x_0, d_0)$ using the EKF.
2. **Read AHU parameters** $(T_s, W_s, C_s)$ supplied by the Coordinator for this timestep.
3. **Linearize** the dynamics to find $A_d, B_d, c_d$.
4. **Construct** $\Psi, \Theta, \Phi, F_{T/W/C}, g_{T/W/C}$ based on the horizon $N$.
5. **Assemble** $H, f, A_{ineq}, b_{ineq}$.
6. **Solve QP:** $z = \arg\min (\frac{1}{2} z^T H z + f^T z) \text{ s.t. } A_{ineq} z \le b_{ineq}$.
7. **Extract** the first optimal control move $u_0^*$ from the top of vector $z$.
8. **Apply** $u_0^*$ to the VAV Box.
9. Store $u_0^*$ as $u_{-1}$ for the next timestep. Repeat.
